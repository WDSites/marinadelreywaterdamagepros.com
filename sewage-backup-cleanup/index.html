Sewage from a backed-up drain is contaminated water, and cleanup means containment, removal of porous materials and disinfecting.

Sewage in the building: what has happened

A sewage backup means wastewater is pushing back up through a toilet, tub, shower or floor drain. The cause is usually a blockage downstream of that fixture. The water carries waste, so it is classed as Category 3, the most contaminated level.

This page covers sewage from the building drain system. Storm and surface flooding are a different cause, and our flood damage restoration page covers that case. If the water has no waste in it and came from a supply line, water damage cleanup is the better fit.

First-hour safety steps when sewage is coming up

  • ✓Leave the affected area. Keep children and pets out, and do not wade through it.
  • ✓Stop running water. Do not flush, and hold off on the washer, dishwasher and showers.
  • ✓Do not pour chemical drain cleaners into a backed-up drain. They can splash and can react with other products.
  • ✓Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners. The fumes are dangerous.
  • ✓Turn off power only from a dry panel. Stay away from outlets near the water.
  • ✓Call a plumber about the blockage and call us for the cleanup.

Do not try to clean it with a mop and household spray. Mopping spreads contamination into places that cannot be cleaned, and rags and cleaners can become waste themselves. Standing sewage also soaks into baseboards, cabinet bases and the edges of carpet within hours, so the cleanup scope grows the longer it sits.

Who owns the line that backed up

According to the LA County Public Works sewer page, sewer laterals are entirely owned by the private property owner they serve. The county maintains public mainlines but not private laterals, and it recommends a backflow preventer and a cleanout in the lateral. For you, that means a blockage in your own lateral is generally the property owner's to fix, and a plumber can say where it sits.

LA Sanitation reports that over 50 percent of sewer backups are caused by tree roots and fats, oils and grease, and that property owners are responsible for the entire private lateral. Work in the public right-of-way needs a permit from the Bureau of Engineering. Tell your plumber if the break appears to be in the street.

In a condo or apartment, the picture is more mixed. Ask your landlord or association who is responsible for each part. If a repair means cutting into walls or floors, confirm which jurisdiction your parcel is in. Los Angeles County Public Works Building and Safety provides permits, plan review and inspection to unincorporated communities, while City of Los Angeles addresses use a different department.

How we clean up after a sewage backup

  1. Set up protection

    Crew members put on protective suits, gloves, boots and respirators. Plastic containment keeps the contamination from reaching clean rooms.

  2. Extract the water

    Extraction pumps and wet vacuums remove liquid and solids, which are bagged and handled as waste.

  3. Remove porous materials

    Carpet, pad, wet drywall, insulation and other soft materials that touched sewage are removed because they cannot be cleaned reliably.

  4. Clean and disinfect

    Hard surfaces and framing are washed and treated with an antimicrobial product made for the job.

  5. Dry and verify

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure, and moisture meters and thermal imaging confirm it is done.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company how its crews apply it to Category 3 water. Be wary of anyone who offers to dry and keep carpet that sewage soaked.

What changes the cost of sewage cleanup

  • ✓The area and number of rooms the water reached.
  • ✓How long the sewage sat before cleanup began.
  • ✓How much carpet, pad, drywall and cabinetry must be removed.
  • ✓Whether a neighbor or a lower unit was also affected.
  • ✓The time needed to dry and clean after the contaminated materials are gone.

Insurers handle sewer backup in different ways. Many standard policies exclude it unless an endorsement for water backup was added. Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies. Keep the plumber invoice and photographs, since they support any claim.

Preventing the next backup

  • ✓Ask a plumber whether your lateral has a cleanout and a backflow preventer, as the county recommends.
  • ✓Keep grease out of kitchen drains and do not flush wipes.
  • ✓Have the lateral camera-inspected if you have had slow drains.
  • ✓Watch for tree roots near the private line on older lots.
  • ✓Know where your cleanout is before an emergency.

Older properties in Culver City and Venice may have aging laterals, so a camera inspection can show a problem before a backup happens. Ask a plumber what your lateral material and age suggest, and keep any report on file with your records for later reference.
