How a rainstorm turns into a water loss

When a storm is heavy enough, storm drains, gutters and downspouts can fill faster than they empty, and the excess goes looking for a way in. It slips under garage doors, through door thresholds, around window frames and down from balconies and rooftops.

Storm water is rarely clean. It carries street dirt, oil and debris, so treat any rain that reaches the inside of a home as contaminated until a crew says otherwise.

Storm damage or flood damage?

The difference matters for both cleanup and insurance. Rain that enters through a roof, window or wall opening is usually storm damage. Water that rises from the ground or flows across it into your space is usually flood. Many policies treat them differently, so check your policy and ask your insurer.

Safety first, before the cleanup

Do not wade through standing water if outlets, cords or a panel could be underwater. Turn off power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If you are unsure, stay out and call the utility.

Keep children and pets away from storm water. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter. Never go up on a wet roof to look for a leak. A roofer can place a temporary cover while we handle the water inside.

What the crew does once the rain eases

  1. Stop further entry

    Sandbags, towels and plastic sheeting slow water at the door. Temporary roof protection is a roofer job.

  2. Extract the water

    Extraction pumps and wet vacuums remove standing water from floors, garages and low rooms.

  3. Remove what cannot be dried

    Soaked carpet pad, baseboard and soft drywall that took contaminated water are cut out and bagged.

  4. Map moisture

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show where water traveled behind finishes.

  5. Dry and clean

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure. Surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and you can ask any company how it applies to your job.

What the creek and recent storms tell us locally

Los Angeles County Public Works describes Ballona Creek as a roughly nine-mile flood protection channel draining a watershed of about 130 square miles into Santa Monica Bay. When a big storm sends water through that channel, low units and garages in the area are the first to feel the pressure. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask your insurer whether a separate flood policy applies.

News reports citing the National Weather Service said Los Angeles had one of its wettest months on record during the 2024 atmospheric river storms. The lesson is practical. Clear your gutters and downspouts before the season, and keep the street-side drain in front of your garage clear of leaves.

News coverage also reported that a 2019 storm filled Ballona Creek near Syd Kronenthal Park in Culver City. That shows how fast the channel can rise upstream, so if you live near the creek, watch the forecast. We serve Culver City and Playa del Rey, where the creek and the lagoon area matter during heavy weather.

  • ✓How deep the water got and how long it stood.
  • ✓Whether the water came from the roof, the walls or the ground.
  • ✓Floor finishes, such as carpet, tile, laminate and hardwood.
  • ✓Whether insulation and drywall behind the walls got wet.
  • ✓Garage contents, stored items and low electrical devices.
  • ✓The number of units affected in a shared building.

Scope is explained before work starts, after someone has read the moisture in your home. A garage that took a few inches of runoff is a very different job from a ground-floor unit where water crossed the living room and reached the wall cavities behind the baseboards. Photos and meter readings from the first visit help everyone agree on what happened.

Insurance, from storm to claim

Many homeowner and condo policies cover sudden storm damage from wind-driven rain, but surface flooding is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Photograph everything before you move it, and keep a list of damaged items.

Storm prep and mistakes to avoid

  • ✓Waiting for the rain to stop before calling, while water keeps soaking in.
  • ✓Running a household vacuum on standing water.
  • ✓Keeping stored boxes on a garage floor in rainy season.
  • ✓Ignoring a small window drip that returns with each storm.
  • ✓Patching a wet wall with paint before it reads dry.
