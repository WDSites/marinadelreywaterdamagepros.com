Why kitchens take the worst of a small leak

A kitchen packs a supply line, a drain trap, a dishwasher hose and often a refrigerator ice line into a few square feet. Every one of them sits inside a cabinet where you cannot see it. A drip from a loose fitting runs down the back of the cabinet, soaks the particleboard base and then wicks into the subfloor.

Many condos and apartments around Marina Del Rey have a kitchen wall shared with a neighbor or a utility chase. According to an encyclopedia summary of county data, the harbor has eight basins separated by six strips of land, and many buildings line those basins in stacked layouts. Water that escapes one kitchen can reach the ceiling of the unit below, so tell your association early.

What you see under and around the sink

Kitchen damage announces itself in small ways. Learn the signs, because early drying saves more material than late drying.

Kitchen water safety in the first hour

Close the shutoff valve under the sink, or the dishwasher or fridge stop if that is the source. If you cannot find the valve, shut off the main for the unit. Kitchens are full of outlets and cords, so do not stand in water near a plugged-in appliance. Switch off that circuit only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.

Empty the cabinet under the sink and set the contents on a dry surface. Mop up what you can with towels, and open the cabinet doors so air reaches the inside. Take photos before you move anything, because they help with a claim. Then call for a moisture check rather than waiting to see if it dries.

How we dry a kitchen

  1. Find the wet zone

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show how far water traveled behind cabinets, under flooring and into the adjoining wall.

  2. Remove what cannot dry

    Swollen toe kicks and crumbling cabinet floors are cut out. Solid base cabinets usually stay in place.

  3. Extract and open up

    Extraction pumps pull standing water. Small holes or removed panels give air access to the cavity behind the cabinets.

  4. Run the equipment

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run in the closed kitchen, with a drying mat or injection system where flooring must stay.

  5. Verify and document

    Readings are repeated until the wood and drywall reach the dry standard, and notes and photos support the claim.

Taken out or dried in place?

Solid wood and plywood cabinets can often be dried in place. Particleboard and MDF soak up water, swell and lose their strength, so a cabinet base made from them is usually cut out and replaced. Laminate flooring rarely recovers from a soaked edge. Tile often survives, but the subfloor below it still needs readings before anyone says it is dry.

Appliance lines cause many of these losses. If a dishwasher, washer or fridge line is the source, the appliance leak water damage page covers that cause in more detail. Once the cabinets and floor are dry, water damage repair handles the new boards, paint and flooring.

Renters, owners and who to tell

Renting is common here. The 2020 census, as summarized in an encyclopedia entry, puts about 69 percent of Venice housing as rented. If you rent, tell your landlord or association before you tear anything out, because the cabinets and floors may not be yours. Ask who handles the repair and who handles the claim.

Tenants in Venice should ask who owns the cabinets before any demolition. According to census data summarized in an encyclopedia entry, about 73 percent of occupied homes in Santa Monica are renter-occupied. Owners and renters alike should document the damage and report it in writing.

Living alone and leaks nobody catches

The 2020 census counted about 49.1 percent of Marina del Rey households as one person. When one person lives in a home and works away from it, a slow leak can run all day with nobody looking. Check under the sink once a month, run a hand along the back of the cabinet and look for water stains. A short check beats a rebuild.

Insurance and common mistakes

Policies often treat a sudden burst or overflow differently from a leak that ran for months. Check your policy, and ask your insurer how it views the loss. Keep the damaged parts if you can and photograph everything before cleanup. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether it works to it.
