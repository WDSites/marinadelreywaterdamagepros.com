Where a water heater leak starts

A leak at a water heater falls into three groups. The tank itself can corrode through, a supply or drain fitting can loosen, or the temperature and pressure relief valve can discharge. A pooling puddle from the tank body usually means the heater is finished. A drip at a pipe or valve is often a smaller repair.

The US Department of Energy says a storage tank water heater lasts about 10 to 15 years. If yours is in that range and water is collecting beneath it, plan on replacement and call a plumber soon. A tank that is leaking from its shell cannot be patched in any lasting way.

Safety before anything else

Shut the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater. Then turn off the power. For an electric unit, flip the breaker if the panel is dry. For a gas unit, set the control to off. If you smell gas, leave the unit at once and call the gas utility from outside. Do not call us first.

Do not touch the heater, the wiring or the thermostat while standing in water. Hot water from the relief valve can scald. Keep children and pets away, and open the closet door only if you can do so from dry footing. The plumber will drain the tank and handle the gas or electrical connections.

Relief valve discharge deserves a note. That valve opens when the tank runs too hot or the pressure climbs too high, and a drain line should carry the water away. If you find a puddle and the valve line is dripping steadily, the heater or the pressure setting needs a plumber. Do not cap the valve.

Where the water goes

Closet floor and baseboard

Water wicks into drywall and flooring first. Baseboard and wall board swell within a day.

Adjoining hallway

Water that clears the sill spreads under carpet or flooring and into the pad.

Wall behind the heater

Shared walls hold moisture and carry it to the next unit.

Floor below

In stacked homes, a ceiling stain downstairs may be the first sign.

Stacked buildings make this a neighbor issue. The Admiralty High-Rise (The Cove) has about 800 condominiums, according to an encyclopedia summary, and sits just across the line in the City of Los Angeles. Any building like that has heaters in closets over other people's ceilings, so tell the manager or association early and ask the unit below to check.

What a cleanup crew does

  1. Extract the water

    Extraction pumps and wands clear the closet and any wet flooring beyond it.

  2. Check the cavity

    A thermal imaging camera and moisture meters show whether water climbed the wall or crossed the floor.

  3. Remove what cannot dry

    Swollen base board, soft drywall and wet pad are removed. Solid framing stays.

  4. Dry the closet and rooms

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run in a closed space. Dry air is pushed into the closet where the heater sits.

  5. Treat the heating element area

    If an electric heater took on water, we leave the electrical parts to the plumber or electrician and dry the surrounding closet.

  6. Confirm dry readings

    We check again until the structure reaches its dry standard, and record it for your claim.

Water from a heater is usually clean at first but can turn dirty if it sits against old flooring or insulation. Replacement, bracing and permits

Replacement is plumber work. California requires water heaters to be strapped to resist earthquake motion, and the LADBS information bulletin calls for straps in the upper and lower thirds of the tank. California Health and Safety Code section 19211 says new, replacement and existing residential water heaters must be braced, anchored or strapped.

Los Angeles County Public Works Building and Safety provides permits, plan review and inspection to unincorporated communities, so confirm which jurisdiction your parcel is in before a replacement. If you live in a City of Los Angeles area such as Playa Vista or on the Culver City side, the permit office is a different one, so ask your plumber which applies.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and homeowners may ask any company how it follows it.

Bracing matters for cleanup, too. A heater that tips in a quake can tear its own pipes loose and flood a closet in moments. If yours has no straps, mention it to the plumber when you book the replacement, and ask the plumber to confirm that the new unit is anchored the way the code requires.

Insurance and utility details

A heater that suddenly fails is often treated differently from long-term seepage. Check your policy, ask your insurer and keep the old tank for the adjuster if asked. In the harbor area, the county-run Marina del Rey Water System has about 300 metered connections, according to Los Angeles County Public Works. Your water bill shows which utility serves your address.
