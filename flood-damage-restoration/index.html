Emergency Flood Damage Restoration in Marina Del Rey, CA

Storm water, groundwater and sewage backups carry contamination, so the cleanup starts with safety and containment, not just pumps.

Flooding in a garage or ground-floor unit right now

Flood water is not the same as a broken pipe. It comes in from outside or from the sewer, and it can carry dirt, debris and contamination into a home. Emergency flood damage restoration in Marina Del Rey is available 24/7, including during storms when many people are calling at once.

While you wait, stay out of deep or dark water, keep children and pets away, and move to higher ground in the building if the level is rising. Lift what you can above the waterline. Do not use the vacuum or any appliance in a wet area, and leave the power off in those rooms unless the panel is dry and safe to reach.

Why flood water needs a different plan

Standard water damage is often a clean supply line. Flooding usually falls into the higher water categories, which means porous materials such as carpet, padding, insulation and low drywall are removed instead of dried, and surfaces are cleaned before drying starts.

Ballona Creek, a roughly nine-mile flood protection channel with a watershed of about 130 square miles, drains parts of Culver City, Santa Monica, Inglewood and Los Angeles toward the coast. Los Angeles recorded one of its wettest months on record during the 2024 atmospheric river storms. Check the FEMA flood map for your address so you know your flood exposure before the next storm.

Residents in nearby Culver City, where Ballona Creek runs through town, face the same question of whether a flooded ground floor is a creek issue or a drainage issue. The answer shapes both the cleanup and the insurance conversation.

Basement and Below-Grade Flooding Cleanup

True basements are uncommon around the harbor, so below-grade flooding here usually means a parking garage level, a ground-floor unit, a storage room or a utility space that sits below the surrounding grade. Those spaces collect water from storm drains, failed sump systems and groundwater pushing up through concrete.

Cleanup begins with pumping out the water, then lifting any flooring and removing wet drywall, insulation and stored cardboard that cannot be saved. Concrete is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers, and the area is cleaned before it is closed back up.

Building owners and associations usually control garage and storage access. Tell them early so equipment and crews can get in, and ask whether the loss belongs to the association's policy or yours. Waterproofing belongs to a different trade, so a flood restoration crew will not promise the water cannot come back.

Sewage Backup Cleanup

Sewage is a safety event

Stay out of the area and keep others out. If water is near outlets or a panel, or you smell gas, leave and call the utility or 911 first. Do not use fixtures that drain to the backed-up line. Call the restoration line after the immediate hazard is handled, and no arrival time is promised.

Sewage backups are Category 3 water. Floors, walls and any porous material the water touched are treated as contaminated. The crew contains the area, removes contaminated materials, extracts the water, and cleans and sanitizes the surfaces before drying begins.

If the backup comes from the building's main line, the association or a plumber has to clear the blockage first, because cleanup cannot hold if the line keeps overflowing. Photograph everything before removal, since the materials will not be kept.
