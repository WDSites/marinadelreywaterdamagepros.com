What a slab leak is and when it applies to your home

A slab leak is a leak in a water supply or drain line that runs through or beneath a concrete floor. The pipe is out of sight, so the first clue is often indirect: warm flooring, a high bill or a damp patch that keeps coming back.

Whether it applies to you depends on the building. Many harbor buildings are multi-story, with concrete floors built over parking, and the pipes in those floors can behave like slab lines. Others run plumbing inside walls and ceilings instead. Ask your association or building manager how your unit is plumbed, or look for a concrete floor with no crawl space below.

A leak detection plumber finds and fixes the pipe. We dry the structure and the flooring that the water reached. If the cause turns out to be a failed fitting in a wall, our burst pipe water damage page covers that case.

Signs the water is under your floor

Warm spot underfoot

A patch of floor that feels warm points to a hot water line leaking beneath it.

Meter keeps moving

If every fixture is off and the meter dial or flow indicator still moves, water is escaping somewhere.

Damp or lifting flooring

Cupping wood, loose tile or carpet that stays damp can mean moisture is coming up from below.

Sound of running water

A faint hiss or trickle in a quiet house, with nothing running, is worth listening for at night.

The EPA says household leaks can waste nearly 10,000 gallons of water per year in the average home. A hidden line leaking around the clock may be a large part of that, so a bill that creeps up with no change in habits deserves a look. Compare a few months of bills, then do the meter check below.

First-hour safety steps and the meter check

  1. Turn off every fixture

    Close taps, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure no one flushes a toilet during the test.

  2. Read the meter

    Find your meter or ask your manager where it is. A moving dial or flow indicator with everything off suggests a leak.

  3. Shut the main or unit valve

    If the reading keeps moving, close the valve for your home. In a stacked building, the riser valve belongs to the association.

  4. Stay off wet floors near power

    Keep clear of outlets and cords in wet rooms. Turn off power at a dry panel if water is spreading toward it.

  5. Call a plumber and then us

    A leak detection plumber locates the pipe. Call us to start drying while the repair is arranged.

Gas lines also run in some slabs. If you smell gas, leave the unit and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Low ground, old marsh and why water lingers here

According to an encyclopedia summary, Venice sits at an elevation of about 10 feet and is very low-lying. A slab that stays damp can take longer to dry than the floor above it, so ask the crew to read it separately. That is why we measure the slab itself and do not stop at the surface.

The same summary says the Marina Del Rey harbor sits on land that was once salt marsh fed by Ballona Creek. Ground that was once marsh can hold moisture. If a pipe leaks into it, water spreads and returns to the floor more slowly than you would expect. Dry readings today do not prove the slab is finished, so we recheck.

Venice itself has a long history. The encyclopedia summary says it was founded in 1905 as Venice of America, its canals were dug to drain marshes and it was annexed to Los Angeles in 1926. If you live in an older Venice building, the plumbing may be original or patched over many decades. Ask your landlord or association what has been replaced.

How we dry a slab and the floor above it

Concrete holds water and releases it slowly. We start with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to map the wet area, then remove flooring that cannot be saved, such as wet pad, damaged underlayment or swollen laminate.

LGR dehumidifiers and air movers then run on the exposed floor. Some jobs use drying mats or injection methods to push dry air into layers. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company how its crews apply it. We return to take readings until the slab is dry. For the bigger picture, see our structural drying page.

What changes the cost of a slab leak dry-out

  • ✓How long the line leaked before it was found.
  • ✓The flooring type, since tile, wood, vinyl and carpet react differently.
  • ✓Whether the water spread into walls, cabinets or a neighbor below.
  • ✓How many days the slab needs to reach a dry reading.
  • ✓Whether the plumber must open the floor, which adds repair work afterward.

Insurance treatment of a hidden leak varies, and many policies separate sudden breaks from long-term seepage. Check your policy and ask your insurer whether the cost of finding the leak and the water damage are handled separately. Keep the plumber report and your photographs.

Mistakes that make a slab leak worse

  • ✓Waiting to see whether the damp spot goes away on its own.
  • ✓Covering wet flooring with a rug or new planks before the slab is dry.
  • ✓Running a fan across wet tile and calling it finished.
  • ✓Skipping the leak detection step and drying without knowing the source.
  • ✓Forgetting to tell neighbors below or the association.

Water that has reached a neighboring home is easier to resolve when people know early. The same early notice helps in Playa del Rey, where low ground and older construction can hide damp for a long time. Owners in Culver City may find their water served by a different utility, so check the bill first.
