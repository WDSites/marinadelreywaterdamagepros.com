When water gets into a Venice Canals home

A failed supply line, a water heater that gives way or a clogged roof drain can put water through a canal-side house quickly. Call us first and tell us where the water is coming from, what it has touched and whether anyone can reach the shutoff. The Marina Del Rey harbor lies a short distance to the south.

We then plan the crew and the equipment around your answers. We do not promise an arrival time, because the honest answer depends on the hour, the call volume and your block. Ask about availability when you call and we will tell you plainly what we can do.

How the canals were built and what that means for a leak

The canals were dug by Abbot Kinney beginning in 1905 as part of his Venice of America. An encyclopedia summary says they were drained and rebuilt in 1992 and 1993 with new sidewalks and walls, and that many large modern houses now stand beside older renovated ones.

That mix matters when we trace a leak. A new house and a renovated older house can have very different framing, plumbing routes and finishes, so we map moisture with a meter before we decide where to open a wall or lift a floor.

Tide gates, low ground and what the city study says

The City of Los Angeles published a Venice sea level rise vulnerability assessment, prepared for City Planning by the engineering firm Moffatt and Nichol in 2018. It describes the area around the canals as low and flat, with ground elevations of about 3 to 9 feet in the low-lying area it studied.

The study explains that stormwater drains to the canals and that the canals are mechanically closed off from the ocean when ocean water levels are high. It records that a tide gate malfunction in August 2017 caused flooding up to the sidewalk in and around the canals area. It also says tidal flooding has mostly been limited by the dual tide gate system.

Most calls we take involve water that starts inside a house, such as a pipe, a heater or an appliance. If outside water ever reaches a building, treat it as contaminated and read about flood damage restoration. We have not verified the FEMA flood zone for these blocks, so look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

If water is still spreading, first safety steps

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff if you can reach it while staying dry. If you cannot find the shutoff, tell us and we will talk you through where it usually is.

Power down with care

Switch off breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise leave the area and call the utility or 911.

Lift and photograph

Raise rugs, shoes, books and small furniture onto counters, and photograph everything before you move it.

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain, a toilet, the canal or the street.

Working on a small, tightly built block

Canal homes sit close together, so a leak on one lot can be a short distance from the next. Tell your neighbors if water may be crossing a wall. Tell us how a crew reaches your door, whether by street, alley or footpath, so we load the right equipment the first time.

Standing water comes out first through emergency water removal. Then structural drying uses air movers, dehumidifiers and moisture meters until the readings match dry reference materials, and water damage repair follows once the structure is measured dry.

Water mains, utilities and who to call

Water service in Venice comes from LADWP. A local news report described a 16-inch water main that burst near North Venice Boulevard and Canal Street and cut service to about 30 homes in the canals neighborhood.

A break in the street is a utility matter. Water that enters your house is yours to dry, so photograph it, call the utility about the main and call us about the building.

Permits and the coastal zone

The canals are inside the City of Los Angeles, so rebuild permits go through LADBS. The city study is titled for the Venice Coastal Zone, so ask LADBS, and City Planning if needed, whether your repair needs a permit or a coastal review before walls are closed. Work that goes beyond like-for-like repair is the most likely to raise questions.
