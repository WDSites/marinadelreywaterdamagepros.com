Water damage in the Oxford Triangle

The Oxford Triangle is a small residential pocket next to the Oxford Basin, with mailing addresses in Marina Del Rey, ZIP 90292. A local news report on a renovated 1954 house there says most parcels run roughly 4,800 to 5,500 square feet, and real estate listings show bungalows, remodeled houses and condominium units.

Homes on lots of that size sit close to their neighbors, so a supply line, heater or roof leak in one can reach the next. Call us and tell us what failed and how far the water has travelled. We do not promise an arrival time, because availability depends on the hour and the call volume.

The Oxford Basin next door

According to Los Angeles County Public Works, the Oxford Basin is a flood control retention facility of about 10.7 acres in the unincorporated community of Marina Del Rey, owned and operated by the county Flood Control District. An encyclopedia summary says it was built in 1959 to prevent flooding in nearby communities.

County Public Works says an enhancement project finished in 2016 modernized the basin, and news coverage describes new tide gates and the removal of about 3,000 cubic yards of sediment. The county land use plan for the marina calls flood control the basin primary mission and places the Marina City Club across Admiralty Way from it.

A flood control basin is infrastructure, not a flood history. We found no source that records flooding in Oxford Triangle homes, so we do not call the neighborhood flood prone. We have also not verified a FEMA flood zone for the Triangle, so check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer how flood coverage works.

Which agency answers for your address

Jurisdiction here needs a quick check. Mail uses Marina Del Rey, but an encyclopedia entry on the marina places the triangle between Admiralty Way, Washington Boulevard and Lincoln Boulevard inside the City of Los Angeles, while the basin itself lies in the unincorporated county community.

County Public Works Building and Safety handles permits for unincorporated parcels and LADBS handles city parcels. Ask the department that covers your parcel whether your repair needs a permit. Your water bill shows which utility serves the address.

If water is still spreading, first safety steps

Shut off the water

Turn the fixture valve or the main shutoff clockwise if you can reach it dry. Tell us if you cannot find it, and we will help you locate it.

Mind the electrics

Never stand in water to reach a breaker or to unplug a device. If the panel is wet or you smell gas, leave and call 911 and the utility.

Photograph, then lift

Take photos of the damage, then raise books, electronics and small furniture out of the water.

Avoid dirty water

Keep children and pets away from water from a toilet, a drain or a storm drain backup, and do not try to dry it with household fans.

A house and a condo need different dry-outs

A house on a lot of about 5,000 square feet usually has its own shutoff, and the repair is yours to direct. A condominium shares risers, floors and ceilings with neighbors and an association, so rules, approvals and building insurance come into play. Tell us which you have when you call.

Standing water is removed through emergency water removal, and water mitigation dries the structure while a meter shows which walls and floors are still wet. If storm drain water entered a garage, storm water damage explains the extra steps.
