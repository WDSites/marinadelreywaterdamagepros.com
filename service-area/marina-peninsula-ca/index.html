Water damage on the Marina Peninsula

The Marina Peninsula lies just west of the Marina Del Rey harbor and south of Washington Boulevard, with Ballona Creek along its southern side, according to an encyclopedia summary. Some listing sites also use the name Silver Strand for part of it.

A pipe, a water heater or an appliance can fail here as it can anywhere. Call us, tell us where the water is coming from and what it has touched, and we plan the crew and equipment from there. We do not promise an arrival time, because availability depends on the hour and the call volume.

Is the Peninsula part of Marina Del Rey?

Not for city purposes. The Marina Peninsula belongs to the City of Los Angeles and falls inside the Venice Neighborhood Council area, where an encyclopedia summary says it also has its own Marina Peninsula Community Council and a property owners association.

A separate encyclopedia entry says the street Via Dolce marks the line between the city and the unincorporated county community of Marina Del Rey. Mail on the Peninsula often reads Marina Del Rey, ZIP 90292, but a mailing label does not decide who issues permits.

Water service and rebuild permits for a Peninsula parcel go through LADWP and LADBS. If your address is close to the harbor side, confirm the jurisdiction for your parcel before you order materials. Our Venice page covers the city departments in more detail.

Lagoon, creek and tide gate: the water around the Peninsula

The Ballona Lagoon Marine Preserve sits beside the Peninsula, and the City of Los Angeles sea level study described on our Venice Canals page says the Marina Del Rey tide gate connects the lagoon directly to the Pacific. To the south, Ballona Creek is a flood protection channel about nine miles long, according to Los Angeles County Public Works.

Those are maps of where water sits, not a record of flooding on your street. We found no source documenting repeated flooding of Peninsula homes, and we have not verified the FEMA flood zone for this neighborhood, so check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer how flood coverage works.

The same city study notes that flooded roads could limit emergency access to relatively isolated areas such as the Marina Peninsula. We mention it because access shapes any job, so give us the cross street and any gate, parking or stair details when you call.

If water is still spreading, first safety steps

Find the shutoff

Close the valve under the fixture or the main shutoff for the unit. In a multi-unit building, ask management where the riser shutoff is.

Keep electricity away from water

Do not touch breakers or plugged-in devices while standing in water. If the panel is wet, leave the area and call the utility or 911.

Protect what you can

Move electronics, papers and small furniture to dry surfaces, and take photos and short videos before you do.

Separate people from dirty water

Keep children and pets away from water that came from a toilet, a drain, the street or the lagoon side.

Everyday leaks that start the same way

In a coastal strip of homes and condos, the sources we are asked about are the ordinary ones. A water heater starts to leak, a washer or dishwasher supply line lets go, a burst pipe opens behind a wall or a roof or deck drain backs up. We have no local data showing which is most common here, so we treat each call on its own facts.

Insurance and paperwork

Photos, a list of wet items and moisture readings help describe what happened. Whether a policy responds depends on the cause and the policy terms, so ask your insurer. Condo owners should also ask the association which parts of the building it insures and which parts belong to the owner.

Water from the ocean or a lagoon rising over the ground is usually flood, which needs separate flood coverage. We explain the scope of work and how the price is set before we start, and we never promise a claim will be approved.

Permits and coastal rules

Small repairs often need no permit, but larger rebuilds can. Ask LADBS whether your work needs one. The Peninsula is part of the Venice area covered by the city coastal program, so ask whether a coastal review applies if the work goes beyond like-for-like repair.
