When water is spreading in Venice

A burst supply line, a failed water heater or a leaking deck drain can put water through a Venice building quickly. Call the 24/7 line first and tell us where the water is coming from, what it has touched and whether anyone can reach the shutoff.

We then send a crew with extraction units, air movers and moisture meters, with same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival time, because an honest answer depends on the hour, the call volume and your street.

Venice sits at an elevation of about 10 feet. That is one more reason to check the FEMA flood map for your address and to ask your insurer how flood differs from a burst pipe before you ever need to file anything.

What goes wrong most often in Venice buildings

Much of Venice is older housing. Many homes are bungalows or early 1900s multifamily buildings, and about 69 percent of Venice housing is rented. That mix means leaks often begin in shared walls, in kitchens sitting above another unit and in bathrooms that were updated around older drain lines.

Older buildings can also hide a slow leak for weeks. A stain on a ceiling in an upstairs apartment may trace back to a fitting behind a wall two rooms away, which is why we map moisture with a meter before we decide where to cut or dry.

Walk streets, canals and getting equipment to the door

Many Venice houses open onto pedestrian-only walk streets. Crews often carry air movers and dehumidifiers in on foot, and that shapes how long setup takes. Tell us about your street layout when you call so we plan the equipment load before we leave the marina.

The canal blocks are part of the Venice Canal Historic District, built on land Abbot Kinney had drained and dug starting in 1905. Low ground is the practical reason canal-side owners should read their policy for flood language and keep a photo record of belongings.

While you wait for the crew in Venice

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff, and leave a note for neighbors in a shared building so the unit above knows.

Power down with care

Switch off breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise call the utility or 911.

Lift what you can

Raise rugs, shoes, books and small furniture onto counters and photograph everything before you move it.

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain, toilet or outdoor source.

Rentals, shared walls and who to tell first

Because most Venice housing is rented, the first call after ours is often to a landlord or property manager. Send photos and the time you noticed the water. Who pays depends on the lease and on how the water started, so keep the conversation factual and in writing.

Owners in small multifamily buildings should also tell the neighbor below. A leak that begins in one unit can wet the ceiling of another within hours, and a second unit means a second set of belongings and a second insurance claim.

How we order the work on a Venice property

Standing water comes out first through emergency water removal. Then water mitigation dries the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers while a moisture meter shows which walls and floors are still wet.

Wet materials that cannot be saved leave through water damage cleanup, and the rebuild follows with water damage repair. If the water came from outside the building, flood damage restoration covers the extra steps for contaminated water.

Water service, permits and the Los Angeles side of the line

Venice is part of the City of Los Angeles, so water service comes from LADWP and rebuild permits go through LADBS. LADBS says each construction stage must be inspected and approved before the work is covered, so ask the department whether your repair needs a permit before walls are closed.

Shops and restaurants along Abbot Kinney Boulevard and the arcaded early 1900s buildings on Windward Avenue share walls and floors. A kitchen line that fails in one unit can reach the next tenant, so call us before the water crosses a party wall.

We also take calls from the Marina Peninsula neighborhood to the south. It is not a separate page here, but the same crews and the same 24/7 line answer for it.
