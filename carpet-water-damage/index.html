Why wet carpet is more than a surface problem

Carpet is only the top of a three-layer system. Beneath the fibers sits a foam or fiber pad, and beneath that the subfloor, which is plywood in many wood-frame buildings and concrete in many towers. Water passes through the carpet quickly and collects in the pad, which acts like a sponge and keeps releasing moisture for days.

That is why a carpet that feels nearly dry on top can still be wet below. Drying only the surface leaves the pad and the subfloor damp, and that is where odor and damage start. A proper job measures all three layers.

Clean water or contaminated water?

The first question we ask is where the water came from, because the answer decides whether the carpet stays.

Water sourceCategoryUsual outcome for the carpet
Supply line, tub overflow, rain through a clean pathCategory 1Carpet is often lifted and dried in place, with the pad replaced
Washer or dishwasher discharge, aquarium, toilet overflow without wasteCategory 2Carpet may be cleaned and dried, and the pad is almost always discarded
Sewage, rising ground water, water that sat for daysCategory 3Carpet and pad are removed and discarded

Clean water that sits long enough can change category, so time matters as much as the source. Tell us how long the carpet has been wet. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and you may ask any company how it classifies water.

Carpet safety in the first hour

Stop the source first. Shut the valve at the fixture or the main for the unit. Do not walk on wet carpet near outlets or cords, and do not plug in a vacuum or fan while standing in water. If the water is dark, smells foul or came from a drain, keep children and pets off the floor and call us before you touch it.

Pick up shoes, boxes and furniture feet that touch the wet area. Put foil or plastic under furniture legs so stain does not bleed into the carpet. Open windows if the weather allows. Skip the household wet vacuum, which pulls up only a little of the water that is deep in the pad.

How the carpet is dried

  1. Test the layers

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera check the carpet, the pad and the subfloor, including areas that feel dry.

  2. Extract the water

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing water out of the carpet and the pad.

  3. Pull the pad

    Wet pad is usually cut out, since it dries slowly and holds contamination. The carpet is lifted from the tack strip along the wet edge.

  4. Dry in the air

    Carpet is propped up on air movers so air flows above and below it, while LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room.

  5. Treat and re-lay

    An antimicrobial treatment goes on the carpet and subfloor, the carpet is re-stretched on fresh pad and the readings are checked again.

Signs the carpet needs to go

  • ✓The water was sewage, ground water or drain water
  • ✓The carpet has been wet long enough to smell sour or show spotting
  • ✓The backing has delaminated and the fibers pull away
  • ✓Stains keep returning after the surface dries
  • ✓Subfloor readings stay high under the carpet

What changes the scope and cost

Square footage matters, but the bigger factors are the water category, the age of the carpet, the pad type, the subfloor material and how many rooms the water reached. Stretching and re-laying costs less than replacing, so a dry-in-place job on clean water usually stays smaller. Delays push a job toward replacement.
