Does Home Insurance Cover Water Damage in Marina Del Rey, CA?

A plain-language look at which kinds of water damage policies often cover, which they often exclude, and how condo owners should read the difference.

By the Marina Del Rey Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

Call (424) 550-5662See Our Services

The short answer: it depends on how the water got in

Home insurance often covers water damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or an appliance hose that fails. It often does not cover damage from a leak that went unnoticed for a long time, from neglected maintenance, or from flooding. Flood damage usually needs a separate flood policy.

That is a general pattern, not a promise. Every policy has its own wording, limits, deductibles and endorsements, so the only answer that counts comes from your policy and your insurer. We are a restoration company, not an insurer or adjuster, and we do not decide what a claim will pay.

Treat that table as vocabulary for a conversation with your insurer, not as a prediction. Two homes with the same flood of water can get different answers because of wording, endorsements or the timeline of the damage.

Sudden versus gradual: why the timeline matters

Adjusters look at how long water has been present. A supply line that breaks overnight leaves a clear event. A drain that has seeped for months leaves staining, rot and sometimes mold, which can look like neglect even when no one knew.

You help yourself by finding leaks early and recording when you found them. Dated photos, a plumber's report and moisture readings give your insurer something concrete to evaluate. Acting quickly also matters for another reason: many policies expect owners to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, so check yours for that wording.

Condo owners: what the association insures and what you do

If you own a condo, two policies usually meet at the wall. The association typically insures the building structure and common areas as its documents describe, while your own policy usually covers your belongings, interior finishes and your liability to neighbors. The dividing line is different in every building.

That line decides who handles a leak that starts upstairs. Ask the association for a copy of its master policy summary and your governing documents, then compare them with your own policy so you know where the gaps sit. If water moved between units, notify both your insurer and the association early.

One question worth asking before a loss

Ask your insurer: "If a neighbor's pipe floods my unit, whose policy responds first, and what do you need from me?" The answer is far easier to hear before the ceiling is wet.

What to document for a claim

  • ✓Photos and video of the damage before anything is moved, showing the source if visible.
  • ✓The date, time and cause as best you know it, plus any plumber or technician report.
  • ✓A list of damaged belongings with approximate age and value.
  • ✓Moisture readings and a written scope of work from your restoration crew.
  • ✓Receipts for emergency steps you took, such as water extraction or temporary storage.

Local note

Marina Del Rey notes: creek water, flood maps and your policy

Ballona Creek is a roughly nine-mile flood protection channel draining a watershed of about 130 square miles, according to Los Angeles County Public Works. That watershed includes parts of Beverly Hills, Culver City, Inglewood, Los Angeles, Santa Monica, West Hollywood and unincorporated county land, and it runs near the southeast edge of the harbor. Storm runoff from all of those places moves toward it.

The practical lesson is to check, not assume. Look up your address on the FEMA flood map to see its mapped zone, then compare that with the flood coverage, if any, on your policy. Ask your insurer whether stormwater entering a ground-level garage or unit would be treated as flood. Owners just upstream in Culver City and across the water in Santa Monica should have the same conversation.
