Mold in Condo Walls in Marina Del Rey, CA: How It Is Found and Removed

Why mold shows up inside shared-wall buildings, what you can safely do, and what removal looks like when a crew sets up containment.

By the Marina Del Rey Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

Call (424) 550-5662See Our Services

The short answer: mold in a wall is a moisture problem first

Mold needs moisture to grow. If you find it on or inside a condo wall, something is wetting that wall, whether it is a slow pipe leak, a failed seal, a condensation line or a neighbor's plumbing. Wiping the surface without fixing the water only treats what is visible.

Small, surface level spots on a hard, nonporous surface can sometimes be cleaned by a homeowner once the moisture is gone. Mold that sits inside drywall, behind baseboards or across a large area is a different job. It is removed with containment and the affected material is cut out. Our crews handle that work as part of mold remediation.

How mold ends up inside a wall

Drywall has a paper face that holds moisture and feeds mold. A leak from a shower valve, a toilet supply line or an ice maker line can wet the back of a wall for weeks without a drop on the floor. Warm, still air in a closed cavity completes the conditions.

Shared-wall buildings add another layer. A cavity may connect to plumbing that serves several units, so the wet area can sit on the far side of the wall from where you see the problem. A musty smell in a hallway closet can trace to a bathroom you do not even use.

The reason for the last row is practical. Sanding or scraping dry mold sends material into the air and into other rooms, which is how a small area turns into a larger one. If you are unsure, leave it alone and call.

Step one is always the water

No mold removal lasts if the wet source is still active. A plumber closes the leak, or the association repairs the shared line, before anyone opens the wall. In a condo, that often means a conversation with the manager and sometimes a neighbor.

Once the source is fixed, wet areas are dried. Our water mitigation service covers moisture mapping, air movers and dehumidifiers, so the wall is dry before it is rebuilt. Closing a wall over damp framing gives mold a second chance.

What removal looks like when it is done right

  1. Assess and map

    Moisture meters and visual checks outline the affected area, including areas you cannot see. Testing may be recommended to guide the scope.

  2. Contain

    Plastic barriers, negative air and sealed doorways keep disturbed material inside the work area.

  3. Remove affected material

    Porous materials such as drywall and insulation are cut out and bagged. Framing is cleaned and dried.

  4. Clean the air and surfaces

    HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping follow. Air scrubbers run during the work.

  5. Verify

    Moisture readings are checked again. Independent clearance testing is sometimes arranged when a lender, landlord or buyer asks for it.

  6. Rebuild

    New drywall, paint and trim are installed, often as a separate step by a repair crew.

Ask whether the company works to the published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation. You can ask any company that question, and the answer should be clear.

Closets, baseboards and the spots people forget

Closets are a repeat offender because they hold clothes close to an exterior or plumbing wall with little airflow. Pull a few items away and look at the bottom of the wall and the baseboard. A bulging baseboard or a line of discoloration along the floor usually means moisture traveled along the framing.

Check behind headboards, bookcases and large wall art as well. Anything that sits flat against a wall for months can hide a growing patch.

If you can see growth on drywall or smell mold without finding a source, call before you disturb the wall.

A note on health questions

We do not diagnose or give medical advice, and no visual check can say what a particular mold means for a particular person. If anyone in the home has health concerns related to the indoor environment, speak with a physician, and tell your contractor about any sensitivities so the work is planned around them.

Local note

Marina Del Rey notes: built on a former salt marsh

Before the harbor was developed, the land was a salt marsh fed by Ballona Creek. Today the harbor has eight basins separated by six strips of land, and most buildings stand close to water on at least one side. Proximity to water is not proof of a moisture problem inside your unit, but it is a reason to take a musty smell on an exterior wall seriously.

The practical step is to check exterior walls, window sills and closets near the basins at least once each season, and to ask your manager how often the building's roof, windows and balcony seals are inspected. If you live in the adjacent Venice or Playa del Rey neighborhoods, the same seasonal check applies.
