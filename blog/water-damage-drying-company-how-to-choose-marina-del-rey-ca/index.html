How to Choose a Water Damage Drying Company in Marina Del Rey, CA

The questions to ask, the equipment to look for and the records a good drying crew leaves behind.

By the Marina Del Rey Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

Call (424) 550-5662See Our Services

The short answer: choose the company that measures

A good water damage drying company measures moisture before it sets equipment, tells you what it is using and why, logs readings until the structure is dry, and puts the scope and price in writing before work begins. A company that promises a number of days before measuring anything is guessing.

Drying is a technical process, and it is easy to do badly in a way that looks fine for weeks. Walls feel dry before framing is dry. Floors look normal while the subfloor holds water. The sections below help you separate real drying from the appearance of it.

What a drying job includes

The work starts with water removal if any is standing. Then the crew maps which materials are wet, places equipment to pull moisture out of materials and air, and checks readings each day. They remove materials that cannot be saved, since drying ruined drywall wastes time.

It ends when readings in the affected materials match those in a dry reference area of the same building. At that point equipment is removed and the rebuild can begin. Our water mitigation work follows this sequence.

You do not need to learn the names. You do need the company to explain each piece in a sentence, to show you where it sits and to tell you why it is there.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • ✓How will you measure moisture, and will I see the readings?
  • ✓What is your dry standard, and how do you set it?
  • ✓How many air movers and dehumidifiers will you use, and how did you decide?
  • ✓What materials will you remove rather than dry, and why?
  • ✓How often will someone check the equipment, and will the readings be logged?
  • ✓Will the scope and price be written and explained before you start?
  • ✓Do your crews work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration?

Notice what is missing from the list: a promised drying time and a promised arrival time. A company that offers either before it has measured anything is selling comfort, not information. We offer same-day response when available, and we tell you what we find before we commit to a plan.

The drying log is your proof

A drying log is a record of daily readings, equipment placement and changes. It shows that wet materials reached the dry standard, and it supports an insurance claim or a later sale. Ask for a copy at the end of the job.

If a company will not show you readings, you cannot tell whether the structure is dry. Moisture left behind is the common starting point for mold, which is a separate and larger job.

What your part of the job looks like

Before the crew arrives, clear the room if you can, take your own photos, and note when the water started. During the job, keep doors to the work area closed so air moves where the equipment sends it, and do not turn off equipment to save noise.

Check in each day. A crew that returns, reads the meters and adjusts equipment is doing the work. A crew that never comes back after setup is not, and you should call and ask why.

Red flags worth walking away from

Be cautious about a company that sets equipment without measuring anything, a company that pressures you to sign a document before explaining it, and one that insists on tearing out everything or nothing. Good crews make decisions item by item.

Equipment that runs for a day and disappears is another sign. Drying often needs several days with daily checks. A noisy house with no one checking readings is a house that is not being managed.

Drying after the water stops, and after the flood

Drying after a pipe leak differs from drying after a storm. A pipe leak is usually Category 1 water and can be dried in place. Stormwater and sewage are higher categories and involve removal of porous materials before drying starts. For those losses, see flood damage restoration and emergency water removal.

Buildings of that age typically contain modern materials such as engineered flooring and laminate cabinetry, which react to moisture differently from older plaster and solid wood. Ask your crew which materials in your home are wet and how they dry.

If drying leads to removing and rebuilding walls, ask that office whether your scope needs a permit. A drying company should be able to tell you which office applies and what documents it will provide.
