What Is Water Damage Restoration? A Marina Del Rey, CA Guide

What the work actually involves, how water categories change the plan, and how restoration differs from mitigation and repair.

By the Marina Del Rey Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

Call (424) 550-5662See Our Services

A direct definition

Water damage restoration is the process of stopping water damage, removing the water, drying the structure and its contents, cleaning and sanitizing as needed, and returning the property to its condition before the loss. It combines emergency work in the first days with rebuilding after.

People use the word loosely. Some mean the quick emergency response, some mean the final repairs, and some mean everything between. Knowing the stages helps you read a scope of work and avoid paying twice for the same step.

Restoration, mitigation and repair: the three words

TermWhat it coversWhen it happens
MitigationStopping further damage: water extraction, moisture mapping, structural dryingThe first hours and days
CleanupRemoving debris, wet materials and contamination, then sanitizingRight after the water stops
RepairRebuilding drywall, flooring, cabinets and paintAfter the structure reads dry
RestorationThe whole sequence from the first call to the finished roomFrom start to finish

On this site, water mitigation describes the early drying work and water damage repair describes the rebuild. Restoration is the umbrella that ties them together.

Water categories: where the water came from

The industry sorts water by how contaminated it is, using categories described in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration. The category sets how crews protect themselves and what materials can be saved.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it means for the work
Category 1Clean supply line, sink overflow, broken water pipeDry and clean. Most materials can be saved if the work is quick
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, some drain overflowsContaminated. Extraction, cleaning and removal of some porous material
Category 3Sewage, storm and surface water, long standing waterGrossly contaminated. Porous materials are removed and areas are sanitized

Water can shift categories as it sits. Clean water that stands for days, or water that crosses a dirty floor, becomes a higher category. That is why the first day matters.

Classes: how much of the building got wet

The same standard describes classes of water damage, based on how much material is wet and how quickly it will release moisture. A small leak into one wall is a lower class, while water across floors, walls and ceilings is a higher one. Dense materials like hardwood and concrete, which dry slowly, can raise the class.

Class affects equipment and time. A higher class needs more air movers and dehumidifiers and more days of monitoring. Your crew should explain which class they assign and why.

The usual sequence of a restoration job

  1. Emergency call and safety check

    Shut off water, check electrical risks and decide whether the area is safe to enter.

  2. Inspection and moisture mapping

    Meters and thermal imaging show how far water traveled.

  3. Water extraction

    Pumps and extraction units remove standing water. Wet carpet and padding are lifted when needed.

  4. Structural drying

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the building. Readings are logged daily.

  5. Cleaning and sanitizing

    Surfaces are cleaned, odor is addressed and contaminated materials are removed.

  6. Repair and rebuild

    Drywall, flooring and finishes are replaced once the structure is dry.

Our crews handle emergency extraction through emergency water removal and the later steps through our cleanup and repair services. The sequence is the same whether the loss is a condo bathroom or a larger flood.

If water is on the floor right now, the first step is to call. You do not need to know the category before you do.

Who does what on a typical job

Several trades touch a water loss, and confusion about who does what causes delays. A plumber stops the source. A mitigation crew removes water and dries the structure. A repair crew, which may be the same company or a separate contractor, rebuilds. An insurer, if involved, assigns an adjuster who reviews the documentation.

Ask each party what they expect from the others. A drying log from the mitigation crew, for instance, is what the repair crew uses to confirm the structure is ready for new drywall.

What restoration does not include

Restoration is not waterproofing. It returns a home to its condition before the loss, and it does not change how water enters a building. If water keeps coming from outside, a waterproofing or drainage contractor solves that problem, and we will say so honestly.

Restoration also is not plumbing repair. A plumber fixes the pipe and the restoration crew handles what the water did. Coordination between the two is part of a good job, and it is worth asking your restoration company how it handles that handoff.

The standards behind the work

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard for professional water damage restoration, and S520 is the equivalent for mold remediation. You can ask any company whether its crews work to these standards and how it applies them to your project. We list them here as the published references, and do not use them to claim a certification.

Local note

Marina Del Rey notes: unincorporated county and the towns around it

Marina Del Rey is an unincorporated community of Los Angeles County, surrounded by the City of Los Angeles. Los Angeles County Public Works Building and Safety provides permits, plan review and inspection for unincorporated communities, and has done so under county building codes adopted in 1933. If your restoration includes rebuilding walls or floors, confirm early whether your parcel falls under the county or a city.

That jurisdiction question changes across short distances. Our crews serve Venice, Playa Vista, Playa del Rey, Culver City and Santa Monica, each of which has its own city or department handling building permits. Ask the correct office whether your rebuild needs a permit before the drywall comes down.
