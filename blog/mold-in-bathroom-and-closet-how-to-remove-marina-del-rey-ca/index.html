How to Remove Mold From a Bathroom or Closet in Marina Del Rey, CA

Shower corners, closet walls and air conditioner drain lines are the usual hiding places. Here is what you can clean, what you should not touch and why.

By the Marina Del Rey Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

Call (424) 550-5662See Our Services

Where mold hides in a home that has no basement

Most Marina Del Rey homes are condos and apartments, so the places that collect moisture are small ones: the corner of a shower, a closet against a plumbing wall, a ceiling near an air conditioner drain, the back of a vanity. Mold shows up there because those places stay damp and get little air.

A homeowner can often clean a small patch on a hard surface once the moisture is controlled. A patch on drywall, a patch that keeps returning, or a smell with no visible source is a reason to call. This guide separates the two so you spend your time on the right ones, and our mold remediation page covers the rest.

Three usual suspects

The first is the bathroom itself. Poor ventilation, a fan that vents into the attic or is too weak, and grout that has failed all hold moisture where steam collects. A line of dark spots along the ceiling edge usually points to ventilation.

The second is a condensate line. Air conditioning units create water, and a drain line that clogs can overflow into a closet or soak the drywall behind it. A wet spot in a closet that shares a wall with the air handler is a classic sign.

The third is a slow plumbing leak, from a sink trap, a toilet supply line or a shower valve. Water travels along pipes and appears on the wrong side of a wall. That is why a mold patch in a closet can be tied to a bathroom next door.

Wear gloves, eye protection and a well fitted mask if you clean a small patch, keep the door closed and the fan on, and never mix cleaning products. If anyone in the home has health concerns, ask a physician before you do it yourself.

A safe way to clean a small hard-surface patch

  1. Fix the moisture

    Repair the leak, clear the drain line or improve the fan. Cleaning first only invites regrowth.

  2. Contain the area

    Close the door and cover vents. Do not dry brush, because brushing spreads material.

  3. Wipe with a damp cloth and detergent

    Work from the outer edge inward, and bag the cloth when you are done.

  4. Dry the surface

    Use a clean towel and a fan. Leaving the surface damp defeats the effort.

  5. Watch it

    Check weekly for a month. If growth returns, the source is still there.

Skip the spray bottles of bleach on drywall. Bleach adds water to a porous surface and does not reach what sits inside it. Detergent and a damp cloth do the useful part of the job on a hard surface.

Drains, fans and the habits that keep shower mold away

Run the bathroom fan during a shower and for a while after it. Wipe down glass and tile when practical. Check the fan cover for dust and listen to whether the fan sounds weak.

For the air conditioner, ask your manager or technician to flush the condensate line on a schedule, and look for a drain pan that has standing water. A line that clogs once will clog again if no one maintains it.

If growth has returned after cleaning, or a wall feels soft or damp, call and we will help you work out the source.

When a closet or ceiling needs more than cleaning

If drywall is soft, swollen or stained, it has absorbed water and needs to come out. That is a job for a crew with containment, and it ends with new drywall and paint. If the cause is a recent leak, water damage cleanup comes first so the area is dry before anyone rebuilds.

Mold clearance can matter if you are selling, refinancing or moving out. A lender or buyer may ask for written proof that the work was completed, so keep every record and ask the company what documentation it can provide.

Local note

Marina Del Rey notes: knowing which water utility serves you

The county-run Marina del Rey Water System serves the harbor leaseholds, with about 300 metered connections and roughly 8,800 people served. Los Angeles County Public Works says most of its water reaches it through County Waterworks District 29, which buys nearly all its supply from the Metropolitan Water District through West Basin Municipal Water District, with emergency interconnections to LADWP. Addresses outside the harbor leaseholds may be served by a different utility.

Mold grows from moisture in a building, not from the quality of your tap water. Still, if you notice staining or smells that seem tied to the water itself, your bill shows which utility serves your address, and that utility can send its water quality report. Ask for it by name and keep it with your records. This applies equally across Culver City and Playa del Rey, where different utilities serve different streets.
